Top 3:

Vivre Sa Vie (1962)

"Vivre Sa Vie" ("My Life to Live") follows Nana, a young Parisian woman who dreams of becoming an actress but ends up drifting into prostitution. Told in twelve episodic tableaux, the film explores her search for meaning, identity, and autonomy in a world that constantly objectifies her. Jean-Luc Godard combines philosophical dialogue, documentary-style realism, and experimental techniques to craft a haunting portrait of modern alienation.

La Haine (1995)

"La Haine" ("Hate") is a powerful social drama set in the Parisian suburbs, focusing on 24 hours in the lives of three young men—Vinz, Saïd, and Hubert—following a violent riot sparked by police brutality. As tensions simmer and violence threatens to erupt again, the film examines the cycle of hate and the harsh realities of poverty, racism, and police oppression in marginalized communities. It's raw, intense, and tragically relevant.

Amélie (2001)

"Amélie" tells the whimsical story of a shy and imaginative young woman living in Montmartre, Paris, who decides to devote her life to secretly helping others find happiness. Along the way, she discovers love in the most unexpected places. With its vibrant visuals, quirky characters, and heartwarming tone, the film is a charming celebration of kindness, fate, and the little joys of life.
